Sites

Build a business a better website before you ever talk to them, then send the owner the link. That's the web-agency loop, and Sites in openleads web is where it lives, from the first click to the email.

#Build one for a lead

Open Sites and find a business (or click Build site on anyone in People). OpenLeads then:

  1. Picks the template that fits what they do: a dental practice, a café, an electrician, a builder, a gym, a salon, a law or accounting firm, and more, with a general local-business template for everything else. The template menu in the site's header switches to any other one, keeping their details.
  2. Fills in their details: business name, phone number, email and city in place of the template's made-up ones.
  3. Has your model write the words for that business, if you've picked one: your Claude subscription (the claude CLI), a key, or a local model. It rewrites headlines, services and the about text, and never invents awards, prices, years in business or reviews: those become short placeholders in square brackets, such as [Your opening hours], and every testimonial becomes [A customer review goes here]. It takes about half a minute and carries on if you open something else.
  4. Shows you the site next to a log of what was done.

No model yet? Use Claude on the Sites screen switches to your Claude subscription in one click. Without a model the site keeps the template's words, with their details swapped in.

#Change anything

Type what you want in the box under the log: "make the headline shorter", "add a section about opening hours", "warmer colours", "use a serif typeface". The model turns the request into Studio's editing commands, and every command is checked against the site before it's applied, so a confused answer can't break the page. Anything refused is listed with the reason. Undo (or ⌘Z) steps back one change at a time, Claude's copy pass and template switches included.

Without a model, Studio's built-in rules handle page-wide requests such as "make it calmer" or "more premium".

For hands-on layout work, Studio in the header opens the same site in the full drag-and-drop editor. Both save to the same place.

#Send it

Send writes a short first email offering the site, addressed to the lead: edit the subject and message, then send. It goes out from your mailbox through the normal sender, so warm-up limits, unsubscribes and the approval rules all apply; if today's warm-up allowance is used up, it waits in Today instead. {{site_url}} in the message becomes the site's public link, and the site is published first if it isn't live yet. A second send to the same person goes as a follow-up.

#The link

Each site gets a preview at /s/<slug>/ on your local dashboard, where <slug> comes from the business name (northwind-dental). The slug never changes once the site exists.

{{site_url}} works in any template or sequence step too:

Hi {{first_name}}, I rebuilt your homepage to show what it could look like:
{{site_url}}

The public link is the Netlify address after you publish, or your own host plus the slug when site_base_url is set (for example https://demos.youragency.com gives https://demos.youragency.com/northwind-dental/). With neither, Send tells you before anything goes out, and a template draft lists site_url as missing.

#Publish

Publish writes the site as a static folder, sites/<slug>/ in your OpenLeads folder, that any host can serve. With a Netlify personal access token in netlify_token (or NETLIFY_AUTH_TOKEN), it also deploys each site to its own free Netlify address and remembers it as the site's public link.

SettingWhat it does
site_base_urlWhere you host published sites yourself. {{site_url}} becomes this plus the slug.
netlify_tokenPublish deploys to Netlify. Stored like your other keys, never shown back.

#The API

GET /api/sites, /api/sites/get?id=, /api/sites/lead?email=, /api/sites/email?id= and POST /api/sites/save, /rename, /link, /delete, /publish, /log, /send, /ai. /ai answers a prompt with the model you picked and nothing else; /send really sends. Same rules as every dashboard endpoint: loopback only, JSON bodies, cross-site requests refused.

Studio's engines (templates, the command validator, the renderer) are also built as one module, /studio/auto.js, which is what the Sites screen runs.
