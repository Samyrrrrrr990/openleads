Sequences

A sequence is multi-step, multi-channel outreach that runs itself — the Apollo model, on your machine. Each step waits a number of days after the previous one, then either sends an email automatically or creates a task for you (a call, a LinkedIn touch, a hand-written email, any action item). People leave the sequence the moment they reply, bounce or unsubscribe.

bash
openleads seq create "Q4 outbound" --pitch "We help clinics fill last-minute cancellations"
openleads seq enroll "Q4 outbound" --list "Q4 dentists"
openleads seq run "Q4 outbound"          # dry run: what would send right now
openleads seq activate "Q4 outbound"     # let the scheduler run it

Or in the dashboard: Sequences → New sequence.

#Steps

StepWhat happens when it's due
Automatic emailRendered for the contact and sent, inside business hours and your warmup cap
Manual emailA task to write and send it yourself
Phone callA call task, with the contact's number one click away
LinkedIn taskA task with their LinkedIn profile linked
Action itemAny other to-do

A manual step holds the contact in place until its task is completed or skipped; then they move on to the next step. New sequences start from a proven 5-step, 11-day cadence — intro email, threaded bump, call, LinkedIn, breakup — that you can edit, reorder or replace.

#Writing emails

Email steps use template syntax, and each can have:

  • A/B variants — every contact gets one variant (chosen deterministically from their email, so a preview matches what is sent), and the sequence page shows sends and replies per variant.
  • Same-thread follow-ups — the step replies to the first email (re: …), so a bump lands in the existing conversation.
  • AI writing — leave the variants empty and each email is written from the contact's details and the sequence's pitch, by the free LLM if you've configured one and the built-in writer otherwise.

The editor's live preview renders any step for a sample or a real contact, with its spam score and a warning for every variable that would come out blank.

#Template syntax

WriteGet
{{first_name}}the contact's first name
`{{first_name\there}}`their first name, or "there" if it's blank
`{Hi\Hey\Hello}`one of the options, picked per contact

Variables: first_name, last_name, full_name, title, company, domain, website, industry, city, country, phone, sender_name, sender_org, sender_context (the sequence's pitch, or yours from Settings) and day. Reusable emails live in Templates.

#Enrolling people

Add people from a list, a People selection (including "all N matching these filters"), the CLI, or an AI assistant over MCP. Enrollment skips — and tells you why — anyone who is:

  • suppressed, marked do-not-contact, or bad data;
  • not verified safe (unless the sequence allows risky emails);
  • already in this sequence, or active in another one (unless the sequence allows overlap).

#Running

Active sequences run on the same heartbeat as everything else (openleads schedule on, or openleads drip by hand): each tick sends the automatic emails that are due and creates the due tasks. Run now in the dashboard (or openleads seq run) does one pass on demand — as a dry run that shows exactly what would send, or live. A dry run works on a paused sequence, so you can check one before you switch it on.

Sends go through the normal sender, so the dry-run default, suppression list, warmup ramp, daily cap, business-hours window and one-click unsubscribe all apply. With IMAP connected, replies and bounces are picked up automatically and take the person out of the sequence.

#Reading the results

The sequence page shows active, contacted, replied (with reply rate) and due-now counts, how many people wait at each step, sends and completed tasks per step, and replies per A/B variant. The Contacts tab lists everyone by status — active, paused, finished, replied, bounced, removed — with bulk pause, resume and remove.
